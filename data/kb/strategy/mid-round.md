## The default

A default spreads the team across the map to gather information without committing. It is not passivity; it is a search. The default ends when you know where the enemy is not.

Take space you can hold, gather information, then commit late with utility intact. Rounds where the team defaults and then hits the same site anyway have wasted the default.

## Adapting

When first contact tells you the site is stacked, the plan changes immediately. Continuing an execute into a known stack because it was the plan is the most expensive habit in the middle ranks.

The information you have at forty seconds is worth more than the plan you made at ninety.

## Lurking

A lurker exists to make the defense hold two places at once. That means the lurk must be noticed at the right moment, not hidden perfectly.

A good lurk takes the flank route, makes a controlled amount of noise to hold a defender in place, and converts when the team commits elsewhere. A bad lurk is a player quietly doing nothing while the team plays four-versus-five.

Time the lurk with the team's commit. Lurking on your own schedule is just being late.

## Reading the enemy

Track what the defense did in the previous three rounds. Repeated aggression in the same place is punishable with utility. A defense that rotated early last round will hesitate this round.

## Rank-specific guidance

In the middle ranks, the gain is ending the default with an actual decision. In the high ranks, it is adapting mid-execute and using the lurk as a timing tool rather than as a position.
