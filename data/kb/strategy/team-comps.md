## The baseline composition

A reliable composition has one controller, one sentinel, one or two initiators, and one or two duelists. The controller is the least optional: a team without smokes cannot attack a defended site at any rank.

Priority when the team is short: controller first, then initiator, then sentinel, then duelist. Two duelists and no smokes is the most common broken composition in solo queue.

## Double controller

Two controllers work on large or narrow maps where space control matters more than entry speed, such as Breeze, Icebox and Bind. Usually one is Viper.

## Double initiator

Two initiators suit maps with hard chokes and predictable defensive positions, such as Split, Lotus and Haven, where the entry needs more than one flash to survive.

## Double duelist

Two duelists work on maps where fast space is decisive and the team can still cover smokes, such as Fracture and Breeze. It fails when it comes at the cost of the controller.

## Choosing within a composition

Pick the agent whose utility the map needs, then the agent you play well, in that order. A comfortable agent whose kit is irrelevant to the map wins fewer rounds than a slightly less comfortable agent who solves the map.

## Rank-specific guidance

In the lower ranks, the only composition rule that matters is having a controller and someone who can flash. In the middle ranks, matching the composition to the map begins to pay. In the high ranks, compositions are drafted against the opponent's likely setup rather than against the map alone.
