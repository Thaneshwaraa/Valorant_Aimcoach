## Setup shapes

A 2-1-2 puts two players on each site with one in the middle. It is the standard because it covers everything adequately and rotates predictably.

A 1-3-1 stacks the middle to take map control early. It wins hard against slow defaults and loses hard to a fast site hit.

A stacked setup puts three or four players on one site based on a read. It wins the round outright when correct and loses it outright when wrong.

On three-site maps, someone is always alone, so the setup question is which site can survive with one player and stalling utility.

## The anchor

An anchor holds a site alone until help arrives. The job is to delay, not to win. Use utility to slow the entry, take one safe duel if it is offered, then fall back and stay alive for the retake.

An anchor who dies on site has given up the site and a body. An anchor who survives has given up only the site.

## Retakes

A retake is an execute run in the opposite direction, and it needs the same four parts: smoke the angles, flash together, enter together, and control the defuse.

Do not trickle in. Three players arriving separately lose to two players holding.

Smoke the spike itself. It denies the post-plant crossfire and forces the defenders to hold close, which is where your flashes work.

Check the time. A retake that starts too late is a fake retake; play for the save instead and keep your weapons.

## Rank-specific guidance

In the lower ranks, the biggest gain is simply not dying on a lost site. In the middle ranks, it is retaking together with utility rather than individually. In the high ranks, it is deciding early whether the round is a retake or a save.
