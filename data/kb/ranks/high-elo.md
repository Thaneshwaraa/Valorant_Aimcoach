## What actually loses rounds at this level

Fundamentals and mechanics are assumed. Rounds are lost to predictability, poor mid-round adaptation, and utility inefficiency.

## The five fixes with the most value

Vary the timing, not just the target. Opponents at this level read patterns within three rounds. The same execute at a different time is a different execute.

Adapt mid-round. When the first contact tells you the site is stacked, the round plan should change immediately rather than continue on momentum.

Spend utility for value, not for habit. Count what each piece of utility bought you. Two smokes that removed one angle is a loss even if the site was taken.

Play the map state rather than the site. Decide based on where the enemy is not, using the information you have actually gathered rather than the site you planned before the round.

Use the lurk deliberately. A lurker exists to make the enemy hold two places at once, which means the lurk must be seen or heard at the right moment, not hidden perfectly.

## What to practise

Review rounds where you won but the plan was wrong. Practise calling mid-round adaptations out loud. Track your utility usage per round and ask what each piece bought.

## Getting to the next rank

The remaining gains are in consistency of decision-making under pressure and in communication quality. At this level the difference between ranks is usually the ability to convert a numbers advantage cleanly, every time.
