## Layout and identity

Lotus is a three-site map with A, B and C. It has three unusual mechanics: two rotating doors that can be spun open or closed, one destructible door at B Main, and a silent drop near A that lets a player fall without making noise.

## Callouts

A site: A Main, A Rubble, A Link, A Tree, A Drop, A Stairs.
B site: B Main, B Pillar, B Link, B Tree, Waterfall.
C site: C Mouth, C Hall, C Link, C Waterfall, C Lobby.

## Attacking Lotus

As on Haven, three sites stretch the defense thin, so probe all three and commit where the count is lowest. Unlike Haven, the rotating doors let you take map control and lock it behind you, which makes slow default play strong.

The links between sites are the real battleground. Owning A Link or C Link gives you both the rotation and a second entrance to the site.

The silent drop is a genuine edge for a lurker: it is the only way to change position without giving audio information. Use it for a late flank, not for entry.

## Defending Lotus

Use the doors to deny an entrance entirely and force attackers into a known path, then stack utility there. Remember that a closed door is also information for the enemy, and that they can hear it move.

## Common mistakes

Spinning a door during a fight and opening an angle onto yourself. Lurking through the silent drop too early, before the team has any map control to convert.
