## Layout and identity

Haven is the only three-site map: A, B and C. Defenders cover one more site with the same five players, so someone is always alone. That single fact drives nearly every strategy here.

## Callouts

A site: A Long, A Short, A Lobby, A Link, Heaven, Sewer.
B site: Garage, B Main, Window, Back Site, B Link.
C site: C Long, C Lobby, C Link, C Cubby, C Window.
Mid: Mid Courtyard, Mid Window, Mid Doors, Tower, Sewers.

## Attacking Haven

The goal is to isolate the lone defender. Spread across all three entrances at the start of the round, find where the single player is, then commit there with numbers. C is often the weakest site because it is furthest from the rest of the team.

Garage is the key control area for B. Owning Garage lets you hit B from two sides and threatens both A and C rotations.

## Defending Haven

The answer to being outnumbered is stalling utility, not aim duels. Every solo player needs a way to delay for four seconds so the rotation arrives. Sentinels are extremely valuable. A 2-1-2 with a roamer, or 1-1-1 with two floaters, are both standard.

Retakes are common and expected on Haven. Save utility for them rather than spending everything on the initial hold.

## Common mistakes

Over-rotating on the first sound and leaving a site completely empty. Solo defenders taking a duel instead of stalling. Attackers spreading out for information and then still hitting the strongest site.
